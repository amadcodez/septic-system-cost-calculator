What a perc test actually measures

A percolation test measures how fast water drains through soil at trench depth — typically 18 to 36 inches below grade. The result is in minutes per inch: how long it takes for water to drop one inch. Slow rates mean tight soil. Fast rates mean permeable soil.

That rate feeds directly into drainfield sizing. A site at 20 min/inch needs roughly twice the absorption area of a site at 10 min/inch, for the same house. At 90 min/inch, a conventional drainfield is usually not permitted.

Perc test vs. soil morphology evaluation

Many states have moved away from the classic perc test toward a full soil profile evaluation. An evaluator digs a pit, reads the soil layers, and records texture, structure, and water table signs. It costs more but gives a more reliable answer — a single timed hole on one afternoon can miss seasonal groundwater that governs the whole design.

When to get one

Before you buy land — if the property needs septic. A result that rules out a conventional system changes what the lot is worth. Discovering this after closing is expensive with no clean solution. Many states require wet-season testing, so check the window for your state before scheduling.

Verify locally. Perc test requirements, acceptable rates, and who may conduct the test are set by your state and county. The figures here are typical ranges — your local health department is the authority for your specific parcel.

Questions

Perc test questions answered

How much does a perc test cost?
A percolation test typically costs $150 to $500 for a basic test, or $400 to $1,500 for a full soil morphology evaluation. The range depends on your state, how many test holes are required, and whether a licensed soil scientist or engineer must witness the test.
What does a perc test tell you?
A perc test measures how fast water moves through the soil at trench depth, reported in minutes per inch. This rate sets the application rate used to size your drainfield — the slower the rate, the larger the field required. It is the single number that controls most of your septic design and cost.
Can a site fail a perc test?
Yes. Soil slower than 90 minutes per inch typically cannot support a conventional drainfield. A failing result usually means a mound system, an aerobic treatment unit, or in some cases no permitted system is feasible. This is why testing before you buy land matters so much.
Do I need a perc test to build a house?
If the property will use a septic system, yes — a soil evaluation or perc test is required in all 50 states before a septic permit can be issued. It is one of the first steps, before any design work.
When should I get a perc test done?
Before you buy land. Many states also require wet-season testing. Check with your state or county health department for the required timing window in your area.
How long is a perc test valid?
Most states accept a perc test result for two to five years. After that, a new test is required before a permit will be issued. If you are buying land with an existing report, confirm it is still within the validity period.
What is the difference between a perc test and a soil evaluation?
A perc test measures drainage speed in a single hole on one day. A soil morphology evaluation involves a licensed evaluator reading the full soil profile — texture, structure, colour, and features that show the seasonal high water table. The profile evaluation costs more but gives a more reliable result. Many states now require it instead of a simple perc test.
