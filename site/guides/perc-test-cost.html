Guide

Perc test cost

A percolation test runs $150 to $1,500 depending on your state and what the test involves. More importantly, the result controls the size and type of septic system you are allowed to build — which is where the real money is.

What a perc test actually measures

A percolation test measures how fast water drains through the soil at the depth where your drainfield trenches will sit — typically 18 to 36 inches below grade. The result is reported in minutes per inch: how many minutes it takes for the water level to drop one inch. Slow rates mean tight soil; fast rates mean permeable soil.

That rate feeds directly into the drainfield sizing formula. A site with a rate of 20 minutes per inch needs roughly twice the absorption area of a site at 10 minutes per inch, for the same house. At 90 minutes per inch a conventional drainfield is usually not permitted at all.

Perc test vs. soil morphology evaluation

Many states have moved away from the classic perc test toward a soil profile evaluation, where a licensed soil scientist digs a pit and reads the soil layers directly. It gives more reliable information — a single hole timed on one afternoon can miss seasonal groundwater that governs the whole design. The profile evaluation is more expensive but it catches problems that a perc test misses.

Who performs the test

Requirements vary by state. Some allow a licensed installer or a health department sanitarian to conduct and witness the test. Others require a licensed soil scientist, a registered professional engineer, or a state-certified evaluator. Your state page names the licensing agency — they can tell you who qualifies in your jurisdiction.

When to get one — and when not to wait

The right time is before you buy land, not after. A result that rules out a conventional system — or any system — changes what the lot is worth. Discovering this after closing is an expensive problem with no clean solution.

If you are planning to build, schedule the test in the wet season wherever possible. Many states require it, because wet-season conditions govern the design and a dry-season test on the same soil can produce a misleadingly optimistic result.

Tests have an expiration. Most states treat a test as valid for two to five years. If you are buying land with an existing perc report, check the date — an expired report means another test before a permit will be issued.

Verify locally. Perc test requirements, acceptable rates, and who may conduct the test are all set by your state and county. The figures here are typical national ranges — your local health department is the authority for your specific parcel.

Questions

Perc test questions answered

How much does a perc test cost?
A percolation test typically costs $150 to $500 for a basic test, or $400 to $1,500 for a full soil morphology evaluation. The range depends on your state, how many test holes are required, and whether a licensed soil scientist or engineer must witness the test.
What does a perc test tell you?
A perc test measures how fast water moves through the soil at trench depth, reported in minutes per inch. This rate sets the application rate used to size your drainfield — the slower the rate, the larger the field required. It is the single number that controls most of your septic design.
Can a site fail a perc test?
Yes. Soil that percolates slower than 90 minutes per inch typically cannot support a conventional drainfield. A failing result usually means a mound system, an aerobic treatment unit, or in some cases a holding tank. On a few sites, no permitted system is feasible at all. This is why testing before you buy land matters.
Do I need a perc test to build a house?
If the property will use a septic system rather than public sewer, yes — a soil evaluation or perc test is required in all 50 states before a septic permit can be issued. It is one of the first steps, before any design work begins.
When should a perc test be done?
Before you buy land, if the property will need a septic system. Many states also require testing during the wet season, because seasonal groundwater governs the design. Check with your state or county health department for the required timing window in your area.
How long is a perc test valid?
Most states accept a perc test result for two to five years. After that, a new test is required before a permit will be issued. If you are buying land with an existing report, confirm it is still within the validity period before relying on it.
What is the difference between a perc test and a soil evaluation?
A perc test measures drainage speed in a single hole on a single day. A soil morphology evaluation involves a licensed evaluator reading the full soil profile — texture, structure, colour, and redoximorphic features that show the seasonal high water table. The profile evaluation is more expensive but gives a more reliable picture of how the site will behave year-round. Many states now require it instead of, or in addition to, a perc test.
