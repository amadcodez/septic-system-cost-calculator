The ground you are actually building on

Karst limestone through middle Tennessee with sinkholes and rapid groundwater movement, shale and sandstone on the Cumberland Plateau, and the west Tennessee coastal plain with loess and sandy soils.

What gets installed in Tennessee

Conventional trenches where soil depth allows. Low pressure pipe and drip distribution are used in shallow soils over rock. Middle Tennessee karst areas face significant restrictions.

Getting a system permitted

TDEC issues subsurface sewage disposal permits and certifies installers. Soil maps and field evaluation determine suitability, and sites over shallow limestone can fail on depth to rock even with acceptable percolation.

  1. Soil evaluation. On the actual field location, before design. It decides what you are allowed to build.
  2. Design and site plan. Tank size, absorption area, and setbacks from wells, lines, water and foundations.
  3. Permit application. Submitted with the soil report. Allow weeks, more in the spring rush.
  4. Installation. By a licensed installer where Tennessee requires one.
  5. Inspection before backfill. Trenches must be seen open.

Drainfield sizing in Tennessee

A three-bedroom home at 150 gallons per bedroom produces 450 gallons per day of design flow. On sandy loam that needs roughly 750 square feet of absorption area. On clay loam it rises to about 2,250 square feet, and on clay a conventional field is usually not permitted at all. Run your own soil class through the drainfield calculator.

Questions specific to Tennessee

Why do middle Tennessee lots fail on depth to rock?
Limestone sits close to the surface across much of the Nashville basin. The rules require a minimum depth of soil beneath the trench bottom for treatment, and a site with only 18 inches of soil over bedrock cannot provide it regardless of how the soil percs. Shallow-placed drip or LPP systems are sometimes the answer, and sometimes nothing works.
Does Tennessee require a soil map check?
Field evaluation governs, but published soil survey data is used as a screening tool. A parcel mapped as having severe limitations is not automatically disqualified, but expect the field evaluation to confirm the problem more often than not.

Verify before you build. The figures above are state minimums compiled for planning, drawn from Tennessee Rules Chapter 0400-48-01, Regulations to Govern Subsurface Sewage Disposal Systems. County and health district rules routinely exceed them, code text changes, and only Tennessee TDEC — Subsurface Sewage Disposal Systems or your local health department can confirm what applies to your parcel.
